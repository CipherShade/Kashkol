# ADR-0010: WhatsApp as a client-side deep link only

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-21
- **Deciders:** Engineering
- **Scope:** M2
- **Serves:** `F-P1-10` (parent updates), `R-03` (no unverifiable delivery claim), `NF-2` (no simulated delivery)

## Context

The teacher needs to send a message to a parent — most likely about attendance, an assessment, or a
homework deadline. WhatsApp is the channel Egyptian families actually use. The product requirement
is explicit about direction and honesty (`R-03`, `NF-2`, and `F-P1-10` AC-4):

- The teacher initiates the message; the platform does not send messages to parents.
- The platform must not claim that a message was sent or delivered.

This distinction is the whole point. A teacher who believes a message was delivered and was not
will chase a parent who never received anything — and the platform would be the cause.

Technically, three levels of WhatsApp integration exist:

1. A **deep link** that opens WhatsApp with a prefilled message. No server contact at all.
2. The **WhatsApp Business API**, where the platform sends messages on the teacher's behalf and
   receives delivery and read receipts.
3. A **webhook-driven** flow where the platform receives message status updates.

## Decision

Implement **only a client-side deep link**. Kashkol has no WhatsApp Business API account, makes no
WhatsApp API call, and receives no delivery or read status.

- The teacher composes a message in the platform and presses a button.
- The platform generates a `whatsapp://send?phone=<number>&text=<urlencoded>` link, with
  `https://wa.me/<number>?text=…` as the fallback for a device without the custom scheme.
- The message text is URL-encoded and the encoding is covered by a unit test.
- The platform records the state machine **`prepared → opened_in_whatsapp`** and nothing beyond it.
  There is no `sent`, no `delivered`, and no `read` state.
- `opened_in_whatsapp` records that the teacher activated the link and WhatsApp opened. It does not
  record that the parent received anything.
- **The UI copy reflects this.** The teacher-facing wording says "opened in WhatsApp" or
  "prepared", never "sent" or "delivered". The copy is covered by a UI test so it cannot drift
  into an overclaim.
- The platform works fully without WhatsApp: no screen requires it, and the message composer is
  independent of it.
- The phone number is derived from the stored parent phone, normalised to international format
  (`+20…` by default), with an explicit choice of format in the composer.
- The preview text is generated by the platform from the message the teacher composed, and the
  teacher may edit it before WhatsApp opens.

## Consequences

### Positive

- No WhatsApp Business Platform approval, no conversation-category review, and no per-message cost.
- No messaging template constraints imposed by a provider.
- No dependency on WhatsApp's uptime or policies.
- The honest state machine means the product cannot overclaim, which is the requirement.
- The teacher keeps full control: the message is reviewed and sent from their own account, in
  their own voice, with their own contact history.
- No data leaves the platform's server in the message path, because there is no server path.

### Negative

- The platform cannot report delivery, so the teacher still has no confirmation that the parent
  read it. That is a genuine product limitation, honestly surfaced.
- The teacher leaves the app to send, and may forget to return; the platform shows "prepared"
  rather than a completed message.
- The teacher has to press send twice (once in Kashkol, once in WhatsApp).
- The number format can be wrong if the stored phone number is wrong; the composer lets the teacher
  edit the number before opening WhatsApp.

### Neutral

- The deep link is a URL, so it needs no special capability, no permission prompt, and no app
  installation.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **WhatsApp Business API (Cloud API)** | Requires business verification, template pre-approval for business-initiated conversations, a per-message charge, and content policies the platform would have to police. It also creates a fundamental conflict with `NF-2`: the platform would be the sender, and delivery receipts would tempt the product to claim "sent" — a claim the requirement forbids unless the flow is genuinely teacher-initiated and receipt-backed. Reconsider only if the requirement changes to permit platform-initiated messages, and only with the cost and policy work budgeted. |
| **Send SMS instead** | Cost per message, poor deliverability to Egyptian numbers compared with WhatsApp, and no thread context. |
| **An in-app messaging system (teacher-to-parent chat inside Kashkol)** | Rejected by the product scope: the requirement is a *handoff* to a channel the family already uses, and an in-app chat would create a support burden and a moderation obligation the product has not designed for. |
| **An aggregator API (several providers, one interface)** | Adds a vendor, a dependency, and a policy surface, for a flow that needs no server component at all. |

## Revisit when

- The requirement changes to allow platform-initiated messages to parents, with a genuine delivery
  receipt.
- Usage data shows the teacher completes the WhatsApp handoff and expects a delivery signal, and
  the business case for the Business API cost can be made.
